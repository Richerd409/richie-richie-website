Strategy for businesses that actually make things

Most strategy advice is written for boardrooms. Tiruppur businesses live on factory floors, in export documentation, and in daily cash flow. Our strategy work respects that reality. Whether you run a knitwear unit deciding between domestic brands and export orders, a trading firm choosing which market to enter next, or a service business that has plateaued — the questions are concrete, and the answers should be too.

What we help you decide

  • Market analysis — who actually buys what you make, at what price, and where demand is moving
  • Competitive positioning — what you can credibly claim that competitors cannot, and how to price accordingly
  • Growth roadmaps — a sequenced plan for the next 12–24 months: which products, which markets, which investments, in what order
  • Diversification decisions — when to add a product line, enter exports, or stay focused and deepen what works
  • Turnaround focus — for businesses under pressure: where the margin is leaking and what to fix first

How we work

Every engagement begins with discovery — we study your numbers, walk your operation, and talk to the people who run it. Then we draft a strategy you can read in one sitting: priorities, timelines, owners, and the trade-offs we weighed. Most importantly, we stay through execution. A plan that is not implemented is just expensive paper, so we review progress with you and adjust as the market answers back.

Because the agency is founder-led, you work directly with the person accountable for your result — no junior team learning on your budget.

Frequently asked questions

What does a strategy engagement look like?

We start with a discovery phase to understand your business, market, and numbers, then deliver a written roadmap with clear priorities, timelines, and accountability. We stay involved through execution rather than handing over a deck and disappearing.

Is strategy consulting only for large companies?

No. Our work is built for SMEs, family-run manufacturers, and growing exporters. The decisions are the same ones large companies face — pricing, markets, capacity, people — just with tighter budgets and less room for error, which makes a clear plan more valuable, not less.

How long does a typical strategy project take?

A focused engagement — for example, a market entry plan or pricing review — typically takes a few weeks. Broader growth roadmaps take longer depending on the depth of analysis required. We agree timelines and deliverables before starting.
